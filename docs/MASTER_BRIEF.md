# VORG-EAVY Master Brief

Last updated: 2026-08-22

## Executive Summary

VORG-EAVY should be built first as a direct-to-consumer fashion micro-label. The broad marketplace idea should stay parked until there is product-market proof.

The highest-probability move is to narrow the system:

- one primary market route
- one brand site
- one drop architecture
- one pop-up format (after the market is selected and permitted)
- one repeatable reinvestment loop

The launch wedge is **U.S.-first**. Per `docs/DECISIONS.md` (2026-08-22), **Brooklyn is the operating lead hypothesis**, while Chicago, Los Angeles, and Atlanta remain inside the engine's one-point co-finalist band. This is an authorized direction and a forecast-only first move, not proven U.S. demand. Canada is secondary: **Ontario -> Quebec -> Vancouver -> Halifax**. Ottawa is not the launch focus.

## Operating Model

VORG-EAVY should sell a world before it sells a catalog.

The public promise can remain:

`A home for Originals.`

The internal operating idea is:

`The Firm.`

The Firm means access, polish, ambition, discipline, and belonging.

## Reference Stack

The strategic reference stack is:

- Luxury restraint: lasting objects, materials, quiet spaces, craft.
- Fashion plus lifestyle: lookbook, sound, store, cafe, editorial, community.
- Youth energy: members, creative platform, emotionally expressive product culture.
- Scarcity mechanics: controlled units, late specificity, local proof, physical event pressure, and sold-out archive.

Do not copy any one brand. VORG-EAVY should synthesize the principles.

## Pop-Up Blueprint

The first pop-up should use two modes in one room:

- daytime: hospitality-led, sparse, premium
- nighttime: access-led, social, content-heavy

Version one should avoid:

- alcohol
- haze
- open flames
- uncontrolled crowd stunts
- vague permit assumptions

Use an indoor venue already approved for retail or assembly use.

## Risk Posture

The first cycle should preserve cash, learn quickly, and avoid legal/operational mess.

Main risks:

- sample delays
- QC issues
- low waitlist
- venue or permit confusion
- crowd management
- high returns
- payment/checkout issues
- HST threshold surprise
- music licensing gaps

Each risk should have a concrete trigger and contingency.
