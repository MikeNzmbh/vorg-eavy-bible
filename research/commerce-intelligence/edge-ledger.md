# VORG Edge Ledger: 30 Tactics

Checked: 2026-07-21

## Decision Supported

Choose inventive but defensible Drop 001 tests that can earn demand proof before VORG-EAVY risks inventory, paid-media, or event cash.

## How To Read This Ledger

- `A` = quantified first-party/platform/vendor case; useful but not independently audited.
- `B` = documented brand/operator mechanism with claimed outcome.
- `C` = credible observation or internal operating principle without causal proof.
- `F` = frontier VORG hypothesis; test cheaply before believing it.
- Risk uses `Green / Yellow / Orange / Red` from the VORG growth risk framework.
- `Now` means eligible once its stated prerequisites exist. It does not mean automatically approved.

Every external result below is a report from the named source, not a promise for VORG-EAVY. DTC Midas scale claims are operator-reported; Shopify, TikTok, Meta, Klaviyo, and Intelligems outcomes are platform/vendor-reported.

## Tactic Cards

### E01 - Comment Becomes The Ad

- Mechanism: Mine real comments, DMs, survey answers, and fitting conversations for one objection at a time; answer it on camera with the physical garment; send the strongest answer to a landing-page section that uses the same language.
- Evidence: `C`. DTC Midas repeatedly emphasizes research into desires, pain points, failed alternatives, and objections, but the public index does not independently verify causal performance.
- VORG mutation: Founder answers `Why this price?`, `How does it fit?`, `What makes it different?`, `Will it ship on time?`, and `What happens if it does not fit?` using only sample/final-product truth.
- Proof: qualified comment rate, PDP click-through, landing-page engagement, and consented opt-ins compared with account/content baselines.
- Success/kill: Continue when one objection treatment beats the median qualified-intent rate by at least 25% across two posts; kill or rewrite after three treatments generate attention but no product question, click, or opt-in.
- Risk: `Green`. Obtain permission before exposing a person's name or message; anonymize by default.
- Source: [DTC Midas public thread index](https://threadreaderapp.com/user/DTCMidas).

### E02 - Personal Story Angle Bank

- Mechanism: Build story ads from researched desire, pain, failed alternative, objection, and transformation instead of generic founder biography.
- Evidence: `B`, operator-reported. DTC Midas says personal-story ads were scaling a roughly US$1M/month brand and publishes a research-first process; no independent audit is available.
- VORG mutation: Five narratives: why The Firm exists, the failed compromise behind one garment, one sample correction, an Ottawa/Gatineau originality story, and the tradeoff VORG refused.
- Proof: three-second hold, completion, saves, qualified comments, PDP clicks, and opt-ins by narrative.
- Success/kill: Advance the top two narratives only if they create purchase-proximate action, not just watch time; kill any story that makes the founder more memorable than the object.
- Risk: `Green`. No invented hardship, customer, or transformation.
- Source: [DTC Midas public thread index](https://threadreaderapp.com/user/DTCMidas).

### E03 - Static Concept Factory

- Mechanism: Separate concepts from executions: one customer tension per concept, then three visual/copy executions. Diagnose the concept before generating more variants.
- Evidence: `B`, operator-reported. DTC Midas reported 159 static concepts in a month and 49% of spend going to statics in a much larger account.
- VORG mutation: Six concepts, not 159: object authority, fit certainty, day-to-night, local arrival, construction proof, and controlled units. Make three variants each from one shoot.
- Proof: outbound click rate, landing-page view rate, cost per qualified opt-in, and comment quality by concept.
- Success/kill: Only scale a concept that wins twice; stop variants when the concept itself fails across three executions.
- Risk: `Green`.
- Source: [DTC Midas public thread index](https://threadreaderapp.com/user/DTCMidas).

### E04 - Viral Format Translation

- Mechanism: Identify the pacing, opening tension, camera grammar, and reveal structure of a proven public format, then rebuild it with original VORG footage, words, music rights, and product truth.
- Evidence: `B`, operator-reported. DTC Midas reported three translated concepts spending more than EUR40,000 each profitably, at a scale far beyond VORG.
- VORG mutation: Translate a format, never the creator's expression. Example: `question -> tactile proof -> fit reveal -> objection answer -> drop action`.
- Proof: hook hold, completion, saves, qualified clicks, and opt-ins versus VORG's ordinary edit.
- Success/kill: Advance only when both retention and purchase-proximate action improve; kill if resemblance is strong enough that attribution or copyright concern is plausible.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Clear music, footage, likeness, and creator rights.
- Source: [DTC Midas public thread index](https://threadreaderapp.com/user/DTCMidas).

### E05 - Organic Winner To Paid

- Mechanism: Publish several native organic posts, identify the one producing product-intent signals, then promote the exact post through the platform's approved ad format rather than reshooting it as a polished ad.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported. TikTok reports CREA Jewelry's Spark Ads beat simultaneous in-feed ads by 44% on conversion rate and 26.4% on CPA; Goodfair reports a 50.7% CPA decrease against its account average.
- VORG mutation: Put no more than C$10-C$15/day for four days behind one organically validated post. Do not boost likes-only winners.
- Proof: cost per landing-page view, cost per consented opt-in, PDP engagement, add-to-cart when checkout is live.
- Success/kill: Continue only if the promoted post reaches a predeclared cost per qualified action and the landing-page view rate is healthy; stop at the spend cap.
- Risk: `Green`; `Yellow` for creator posts unless boosting permission, usage term, and disclosure are documented.
- Sources: [CREA Jewelry](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/crea-jewelry) and [Goodfair](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/inspiration/goodfair-466).

### E06 - Native 9:16 Reels Cell

- Mechanism: Compare a native 9:16, sound-on, safe-zone edit with the closest possible non-native or static control.
- Evidence: `A`, platform-reported. Meta cites a 15-test analysis in which native 9:16 Reels creative had 34.5% lower cost per result than image ads in Reels; a separate 15-test analysis reported 5% lower cost per result and 11% higher conversion after adding partner-enabled native Reels creative.
- VORG mutation: One 12-20 second object-first cut with captions, product in frame immediately, tactile audio, and one action.
- Proof: cost per qualified action and landing-page view rate; do not use view count as the decision metric.
- Success/kill: Require a minimum sample sufficient for a directional comparison; stop if the native edit produces cheaper clicks but worse on-site intent.
- Risk: `Green`.
- Source: [Meta Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads).

### E07 - Creator Partnership Cluster

- Mechanism: Give several small aligned creators one product tension and freedom to produce in their native language; pay or gift transparently; promote only posts that earn real product intent.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported. TikTok reports FashionZone's creator partnership/Spark Ads program reduced CPA 21%, CPM 47%, and CPC 32%.
- VORG mutation: Three Ottawa/Gatineau connectors, one sample rotation, three distinct contexts, no scripts or praise requirement.
- Proof: qualified comments, profile/PDP clicks, opt-ins, and cost per qualified action after any promotion.
- Success/kill: Continue a creator relationship only when product-intent quality and content reusability justify cost; do not rank creators by follower count alone.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Written usage rights, gifting/payment disclosure, consent, and return/care terms for samples.
- Source: [FashionZone](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/fashionzone-tiktok-success-story).

### E08 - Live Ad Radar

- Mechanism: Observe ads that remain live or recur, then code hook, promise, proof, format, offer, landing destination, and date. Longevity is a prioritization clue, not profitability proof.
- Evidence: `C`. Meta and TikTok provide official public discovery tools; neither exposes an advertiser's profit.
- VORG mutation: Weekly 100-ad scan across fashion, accessories, local culture, luxury, and adjacent categories; extract ten mechanisms and three VORG mutations.
- Proof: percentage of scan-derived concepts that become testable and percentage that beat VORG baselines.
- Success/kill: Keep only fields that inform decisions; remove screenshots or copied assets that have no mechanism note.
- Risk: `Green` when manually observed or accessed through permitted tools. Unauthorized scraping is `Red`.
- Sources: [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) and [TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/tiktok-creative-center).

### E09 - The Firm Referral Ladder

- Mechanism: Give every consented waitlist member a unique link and a visible progression of achievable rewards for verified referrals.
- Evidence: `A`, first-person case. Harry's co-founder reported nearly 100,000 emails in a one-week prelaunch using a two-page referral flow and escalating tangible rewards. That result came from a larger team/network and is not a VORG benchmark.
- VORG mutation: 3 referrals = digital product dossier; 5 = reserved pop-up fitting; 10 = numbered print; 20 = consented founder portrait. Avoid free garments until unit economics support them.
- Proof: invited users, verified referred opt-ins, K-factor, reward liability, and percentage of referrals who show product intent.
- Success/kill: Continue if at least 20% of seeded members share and referred leads show comparable or better intent than direct leads; kill if fraud, low-quality signups, or reward liability exceeds the approved cap.
- Risk: `Yellow`. CASL consent and referral rules apply; the brand should capture the new person's consent on the landing page rather than auto-emailing referred addresses.
- Sources: [Harry's prelaunch case](https://tim.blog/2014/07/21/harrys-prelaunchr-email/) and [CRTC CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm).

### E10 - Traveling Firm Jacket

- Mechanism: One credible sample moves through seven aligned Ottawa/Gatineau people in seven days. Each person styles it, answers one honest product question, and nominates the next carrier.
- Evidence: `F`. This is a VORG mutation of genuine seeding, relay narratives, and local social proof; it has not been tested.
- VORG mutation: Publish a chain map and a daily proof card. The product, not the connector's fame, stays central.
- Proof: completed handoffs, qualified product questions, waitlist clicks, fit notes, reusable assets, sample condition.
- Success/kill: Success requires at least five completed handoffs, ten qualified product questions, three consented reusable assets, and two purchase-intent statements; stop immediately for sample damage, unsafe handoff, non-consent, or weak product visibility.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Written custody, cleaning/care, usage rights, disclosure, transport, and face consent.
- Source basis: VORG frontier hypothesis informed by the real-sightings system in `launch/campaign-proof-playbook.md`.

### E11 - Controlled Garment Exchange

- Mechanism: People bring a defined clean garment; a limited group receives a fitting, repair/alteration credit, purchase privilege, or controlled VORG item; collected pieces follow a verified donation, repair, or upcycle path.
- Evidence: `C`. Vogue reported hundreds attending Corteiz's 50-jacket Bolo Exchange; GQ documented roughly 400 people and a 300-person denim exchange in Brooklyn. These observations prove attention, not profit, and also show crowd risk.
- VORG mutation: RSVP-only, disclosed capacity, timed arrivals, known venue, no races, no surprise shortage, and a confirmed material destination.
- Proof: qualified RSVPs, attendance, opt-ins, product questions, sell-through, reusable content, and partner receipt.
- Success/kill: Proceed only with venue, queue lead, security, terms, and partner; cancel if the mechanic sounds like a free-product bait or demand exceeds safe capacity.
- Risk: `Yellow`, potentially `Orange` without crowd and venue controls.
- Sources: [Vogue on the Bolo Exchange](https://www.vogue.com/article/corteiz-clint-and-the-future-of-streetwear) and [GQ on the Brooklyn Denim Exchange](https://www.gq.com/story/corteiz-brooklyn-denim-exchange-scene-report).

### E12 - Permissioned City Clue Trail

- Mechanism: A sequence of approved partner locations reveals product details, story fragments, or a fitting/RSVP action; each QR is location-specific.
- Evidence: `C`. Corteiz used late coordinates and clues to mobilize audiences, but observed executions also created unsafe movement.
- VORG mutation: Known operating hours, no race, no first-come product prize, permissioned displays only, and a digital completion path.
- Proof: scans, opt-ins, completion rate, product-detail recall, and partner satisfaction by location.
- Success/kill: Keep a location only when at least 20% of scans convert to a qualified opt-in or RSVP; stop if people trespass, litter, run, or misunderstand the action.
- Risk: `Yellow`.
- Sources: [Vogue](https://www.vogue.com/article/corteiz-clint-and-the-future-of-streetwear) and [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/corteiz-brooklyn-denim-exchange-scene-report).

### E13 - Pay With Originality

- Mechanism: A person completes a short, transparent creative act to unlock a controlled benefit; participation itself becomes content and customer qualification.
- Evidence: `A-to-F`. Shopify reports Endorphins' `Pay with Your Pace` drew 646 participants, 67% of treadmill sessions led immediately to purchase, and 94% were new to its Shopify store. The VORG originality mutation is untested.
- VORG mutation: Submit or perform 30 seconds of original work to unlock one approved benefit: portrait, styling session, numbered card, fitting priority, or tightly capped store credit.
- Proof: participants, consented opt-ins, purchase/RSVP conversion, content permissions, and benefit cost.
- Success/kill: Continue if at least 40% complete the product/RSVP action and the content is usable; stop if judging feels arbitrary, exploitative, humiliating, or disconnected from product.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Publish skill criteria, benefit value, capacity, content rights, privacy treatment, and accessibility alternative. If chance is involved, contest rules need review.
- Source: [Endorphins case](https://www.shopify.com/no-en/case-studies/endorphins).

### E14 - Skill-Shot Store Credit

- Mechanism: A customer who meets a disclosed spend threshold gets one transparent skill attempt to earn a defined credit, turning purchase into live theatre.
- Evidence: `A-to-F`. Shopify reports 100 Culture Kings customers completed its basketball `Double or Nothing Challenge`; the VORG version has no proof.
- VORG mutation: Use a low-risk originality or styling decision, not athletics, gambling language, or an all-or-nothing loss. The original purchase remains intact.
- Proof: eligible participants, participation rate, incremental basket, redeemed credit, UGC permissions, and queue time.
- Success/kill: Kill if the benefit math threatens margin, the rules create embarrassment, or the queue harms checkout.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Written skill rules, prize/credit value, eligibility, accessibility path, liability review, and no deceptive `nothing` outcome.
- Source: [Culture Kings case](https://www.shopify.com/ie/case-studies/culture-kings-black-friday).

### E15 - Time-Boxed Demand Window

- Mechanism: Open a genuine preorder window with disclosed delivery range, refund/cancellation terms, and no arbitrary unit cap; close it at the stated time.
- Evidence: `B`. Telfar documents that its Bag Security Program allowed preorder of any colour, size, and quantity during a limited event, with shipment three to six months later.
- VORG mutation: Use only for a product with signed vendor quote, tested sample, confirmed MOQ, production capacity, QC plan, and conservative ship window. No password gate.
- Proof: paid demand by size, refund requests, support volume, fulfilment variance, and contribution after financing/refunds.
- Success/kill: Do not launch without vendor and cash-flow proof; pause if order count exceeds confirmed capacity or the delivery promise changes materially.
- Risk: `Yellow`, potentially `Orange` when delivery or refund capacity is uncertain.
- Source: [Telfar general questions](https://help.telfar.net/en-US/articles/general-questions-55282).

### E16 - Batch Restock Release

- Mechanism: Notify small batches of back-in-stock subscribers, then release the next batch only if inventory remains. This reduces the `email arrived but already sold out` failure.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported. Klaviyo says Brava Fabrics used small batches such as ten people and identified back-in-stock as a top flow; its broader case reports automated emails were 60% of email revenue.
- VORG mutation: Sequence exact SKU/size subscribers by signup time, with sold/held/available inventory kept accurate.
- Proof: batch conversion, time-to-sell, unsubscribes, complaints, and unsold units after each interval.
- Success/kill: Widen the next batch only if inventory remains and support is stable; stop all sends at zero available units.
- Risk: `Green` with valid consent and accurate inventory.
- Source: [Brava Fabrics case](https://www.klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/brava-fabrics).

### E17 - Threshold-Backed Demand Guarantee

- Mechanism: When a real size/SKU sells out, collect paid preorders only for a disclosed second-run threshold; automatically refund if the threshold is not met by the stated date.
- Evidence: `F`. This combines threshold funding and preorder logic; it has no VORG result.
- VORG mutation: `If 12 verified orders for this size run are reached by DATE, production starts for delivery WINDOW; otherwise every order is refunded by DATE.` Numbers are placeholders until the vendor confirms them.
- Proof: paid threshold progress, refund rate, support load, contribution margin, and vendor acceptance.
- Success/kill: Never announce before MOQ, costs, production slot, payment/refund mechanics, and fulfilment buffer are verified; kill at the deadline if the exact threshold is missed.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Guarantee wording must be genuine and operationally fundable; false urgency or an impossible guarantee is prohibited.
- Source basis: frontier mutation; see [Competition Bureau guarantee and fake-urgency surfaces](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/misleading-representations-and-deceptive-marketing-practices).

### E18 - Mystery Styling Bundle

- Mechanism: Sell a bounded-surprise bundle where category, count, sizing method, condition, exclusions, value logic, returns, and fulfilment are disclosed before purchase.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported. TikTok reports Goodfair's mystery thrift bundles plus Spark Ads produced 885,000 views and a 50.7% CPA decrease versus account average.
- VORG mutation: `The Firm Styling File`: one owned item plus a disclosed mix of non-inventory editorial/styling artifacts; do not bury weak stock or hide material terms.
- Proof: bundle conversion, contribution, support contacts, disappointment/refund rate, content output, and repeat intent.
- Success/kill: Kill if surprise makes sizing or value ambiguous, creates return risk, or harms luxury positioning.
- Risk: `Yellow`.
- Source: [Goodfair case](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/inspiration/goodfair-466).

### E19 - Founder Delivery Documentary

- Mechanism: Offer the first few local customers an optional founder delivery and short fit/reaction capture, while providing an equivalent ordinary delivery path.
- Evidence: `F`. This is an untested VORG trust and local-proof hypothesis.
- VORG mutation: Five Ottawa/Gatineau orders maximum; pre-booked time; no surprise arrival; content optional and separately consented.
- Proof: delivery acceptance, fit insight, content permission, referral/UGC, delivery cost and time.
- Success/kill: Continue only if it produces high-trust evidence without slowing normal fulfilment; stop for privacy, safety, schedule, or unequal-service concern.
- Risk: `Yellow`.
- Source basis: VORG frontier hypothesis.

### E20 - Pop-Up Proof Engine

- Mechanism: Design every station to convert, teach, or capture evidence: fit station, founder table, objection station, originality action, real sold/held/available wall, next-city vote, and checkout/opt-in.
- Evidence: `C`. The components borrow documented experiential and social-proof mechanisms, but the combined VORG system has no causal proof.
- VORG mutation: Rehearse the room with 12-20 invited people before committing full event spend. Capture where people stop, ask, try, opt in, and abandon.
- Proof: station dwell, fit completion, objection resolution, RSVP/opt-in/purchase action, content permissions, queue time, and staff accuracy.
- Success/kill: A station stays only if it causes a measurable action or produces a required asset; remove confusing theatre. No fake receipts, fake line, or moved `sold` markers.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Venue, capacity, accessibility, photography consent, music, security, POS, and inventory truth.
- Source basis: VORG synthesis of [Endorphins](https://www.shopify.com/no-en/case-studies/endorphins), [Culture Kings](https://www.shopify.com/ie/case-studies/culture-kings-black-friday), and `launch/campaign-proof-playbook.md`.

### E21 - Ad-To-Page Continuity

- Mechanism: The landing page repeats the ad's exact product, promise, proof, imagery, and next action above the fold. One ad concept gets one matching page module or anchored destination.
- Evidence: `C`. This is a recurring operator/CRO principle; VORG has no baseline yet.
- VORG mutation: Five modular page openings aligned to object authority, fit, construction, local arrival, and day-to-night styling.
- Proof: landing-page view rate, bounce/engagement, scroll to proof, opt-in/add-to-cart, and support questions by source concept.
- Success/kill: A winning ad with weak on-site action triggers a continuity diagnosis before more media spend; kill mismatched creative/page pairs.
- Risk: `Green`.
- Source basis: DTC Midas operator material and VORG conversion hypothesis; validate against VORG data.

### E22 - Shipping Threshold Test

- Mechanism: Test one economically viable free-shipping threshold against the current offer while measuring conversion, AOV, units/order, revenue/visitor, contribution, and return effects.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported. Intelligems reports one brand's tested threshold increased units/order 6.3%, AOV 6.5%, and monthly revenue about 3%, alongside roughly 3.5% conversion impact.
- VORG mutation: Do not guess a threshold. Calculate it from real carrier quotes, packaging, geography, margin, and likely baskets; use a single test only after adequate traffic.
- Proof: contribution per visitor is the decision metric, not AOV alone.
- Success/kill: Adopt only if contribution per visitor improves without unacceptable conversion or support damage; kill if mandatory fees make the advertised price unattainable.
- Risk: `Green` with transparent pricing; drip pricing is prohibited.
- Sources: [Intelligems shipping case](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/customer-stories/testing-your-free-shipping-threshold-can-improve-aov-and-revenue) and [Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/misleading-representations-and-deceptive-marketing-practices).

### E23 - Price And Offer Cell

- Mechanism: Test price, bundle, discount, shipping, or return-policy hypotheses one variable at a time against contribution per visitor.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported. Intelligems says more than 80% of participating OpenStore brands saw profit uplift, averaging 13%; this aggregate is not an independent benchmark and may not transfer.
- VORG mutation: First test is qualitative price acceptance against the vendor-quoted floor; a live A/B price test waits for enough traffic and inventory truth.
- Proof: contribution per visitor, conversion, gross profit/order, return intent, and customer confusion.
- Success/kill: Never lower price below approved contribution floor; never advertise an inflated ordinary price or fake discount.
- Risk: `Yellow` because inconsistent prices and reference-price claims can create trust or compliance issues.
- Sources: [Intelligems pricing case](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/blog/getting-scientific-with-pricing) and [Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/misleading-representations-and-deceptive-marketing-practices).

### E24 - Size-Safe Complete-Look Bundle

- Mechanism: Pair a high-fit-risk hero with a low-fit-risk accessory or service benefit, and show the exact economics instead of an invented markdown.
- Evidence: `C`. Bundle testing is documented in commerce practice, but this VORG combination is untested.
- VORG mutation: Jacket + scarf, or denim + fitting/alteration credit, only after landed costs and return liability are known.
- Proof: attach rate, contribution/order, return rate, and percentage buying only for the discount.
- Success/kill: Adopt only if contribution and product comprehension improve; kill if the bundle obscures individual value or creates dead stock.
- Risk: `Green`; `Yellow` if discount/reference-price claims are used.
- Source basis: VORG offer hypothesis; `E23` governs live testing.

### E25 - Fit Proof Relay

- Mechanism: Show the same SKU on several relevant bodies; record body/garment measurements, worn size, movement, comfort, and honest fit notes; connect every asset to the size guide.
- Evidence: `C`. Strong product-risk logic, but no VORG result exists.
- VORG mutation: Five to eight target bodies for each high-risk size architecture, with no claim that one sample represents all production units.
- Proof: size-confidence responses, size-guide usage, product questions, add-to-cart, exchanges, and returns by fit reason.
- Success/kill: Bulk approval should stop if fit cannot be explained consistently or the sample fails target bodies.
- Risk: `Green` with consent, inclusive treatment, and accurate measurements.
- Source basis: `launch/campaign-proof-playbook.md`.

### E26 - Intent-Split Welcome Flow

- Mechanism: Ask every subscriber what they want—product/size, pop-up, city, or founder/process—and tailor the welcome path while keeping the commercial consent explicit.
- Evidence: `C`. Behavioural segmentation and automated flows are documented, but VORG has no result.
- VORG mutation: Email 1 delivers the promised asset; email 2 gives product proof; email 3 answers the chosen objection; email 4 states the real launch action.
- Proof: consent completion, preference capture, click-to-proof, unsubscribe, reply quality, and downstream purchase/RSVP.
- Success/kill: Remove segments that do not change content or decisions; stop sequences with high complaint/unsubscribe rates.
- Risk: `Green` when express consent, identity, unsubscribe, and consent records are present.
- Sources: [Brava Fabrics case](https://www.klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/brava-fabrics) and [CRTC CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm).

### E27 - Consent-Safe Recovery Flow

- Mechanism: Recover browse/cart/checkout intent only for people with a documented permission path; lead with the unresolved product question, not escalating fake urgency.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported at the system level. Klaviyo reports Brava Fabrics' automated flows produced 60% of its email revenue, including behaviour-triggered flows; it does not isolate a VORG-equivalent result.
- VORG mutation: One fit reminder, one product-proof reminder, and one real deadline/inventory update only when true.
- Proof: recovered orders, contribution, unsubscribes, complaints, and support contacts.
- Success/kill: Stop a branch if it creates complaints or depends on implied consent from an abandoned checkout alone.
- Risk: `Yellow`. The CRTC warns that abandoned checkout may not create implied consent and recommends express consent.
- Sources: [Brava Fabrics](https://www.klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/brava-fabrics) and [CRTC CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm).

### E28 - Review-To-Service-To-Advocacy

- Mechanism: Ask for structured post-purchase feedback; route dissatisfied customers to service before promotion; make it easy for genuine advocates to share without scripting praise.
- Evidence: `A`, vendor-reported. Klaviyo documents Brava Fabrics using review-triggered paths for advocacy and recovery, though the case does not prove the isolated tactic caused its total growth.
- VORG mutation: Capture fit, quality, delivery, and expectation match. Resolve low scores personally; offer satisfied customers an optional share/referral tool with disclosure where benefit exists.
- Proof: response rate, issues resolved, verified review rate, referral action, repeat purchase, and return reasons.
- Success/kill: Never suppress valid negative reviews, condition service on review removal, or fabricate/reshape testimonials.
- Risk: `Yellow` because testimonials, incentives, privacy, and disclosures must be accurate.
- Source: [Brava Fabrics case](https://www.klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/brava-fabrics).

### E29 - Post-Purchase Styling Mission

- Mechanism: Give buyers a seven-day styling prompt and optional submission path; publish only consented work; reward contribution with access/content/status before discounts.
- Evidence: `F`. This VORG retention/UGC mechanism is untested.
- VORG mutation: `One object, two lives`: show the piece in a work/day setting and a night/creative setting; capture fit notes alongside the look.
- Proof: participation, consented assets, product questions generated, referral clicks, repeat intent, and moderation cost.
- Success/kill: Continue only when the product remains central and participants feel credited; stop if pressure, comparison, or reward ambiguity harms trust.
- Risk: `Yellow`. Clear content licence, credit preference, eligibility, and disclosure.
- Source basis: VORG frontier hypothesis.

### E30 - Next-City Signal Market

- Mechanism: Let people allocate one verified vote to a city, then require a consented city-specific signup or refundable reservation before treating the signal as demand.
- Evidence: `C`. Voting is a useful segmentation device but weak demand proof unless paired with costly action.
- VORG mutation: Ottawa/Gatineau remains the wedge; Montreal, Toronto, Vancouver, and Halifax compete on verified opt-ins, qualified purchase intent, and later paid reservations—not likes.
- Proof: unique city signups, referral-adjusted growth, purchase-intent response, cost to serve, and duplicate/fraud rate.
- Success/kill: Do not announce a next city from a poll. Require a founder-approved threshold based on event and fulfilment economics.
- Risk: `Green`; `Yellow` if money is collected before terms/refunds and delivery are settled.
- Source basis: `launch/campaign-proof-playbook.md`.

## Portfolio Rules

1. Run one primary variable per test. Multiple assets may express it, but the decision must be singular.
2. Preserve at least 50% of the C$450 paid-social/seeding budget until a creative and landing path earn an organic or low-cost signal.
3. No more than 10% of launch marketing resources go to unsupported frontier tactics before proof.
4. Do not scale a tactic on views, likes, queue size, or press alone. Require a purchase-proximate signal.
5. Do not port spend structures from a US$1M/month operator account into a first Ottawa/Gatineau drop.
6. All scarcity, inventory, deadlines, testimonials, queues, receipts, holds, and sell-outs must be real and auditable.
7. A tactic that wins attention but damages fulfilment, fit confidence, contribution, or trust loses.
