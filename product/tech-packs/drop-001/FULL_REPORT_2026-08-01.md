# VORG-EAVY Drop 001: Full Tech-Pack, Sourcing, and Buy Decision Report

**Prepared:** 2026-08-01  
**Decision supported:** What to sample, where to source it, how to protect a C$6,000 landed-clothing cash ceiling, and what evidence must exist before any bulk order.  
**Truth level:** Founder-review RFQ and prototype specification.  
**Production status:** **Not production-ready.** No supplier has passed the evidence, sample, fit, testing, quotation, inspection, and landed-cost gates in this report.

## Executive verdict

VORG-EAVY should use **China for a controlled sampling tournament**, not give China or any marketplace seller blanket approval. The first-round structure should be:

- a maximum of **two production relationships** in one South China consolidation lane;
- one proven denim specialist for the separate women's and men's jean blocks;
- one proven jacket/top factory for The Firm Jacket and fitted long-sleeve top;
- a ready-stock scarf with a custom label only if fibre, pilling, shedding, and colour evidence pass;
- bulk only the **two strongest products**, with a third allowed only if demand, fit, MOQ, timing, and the landed cash model all clear;
- keep the C$6,000 as a **landed-to-Ottawa cash ceiling**, not a factory-invoice budget.

Diversifying across China, Portugal, and Canada would be strategically attractive later, but it is inefficient at this cash level. It fragments small orders, creates several sample and freight streams, weakens negotiating leverage, and consumes the money needed for inspection and contingency. Capability diversification inside one manufacturing hub is the better risk trade.

The founder's latest jacket decision is locked into v0.2: **black leather collar; no cuff component; no wrist taper or clinch; the quilted sleeve runs continuously to the wrist and ends with a 3 cm self-fabric turnback and clean parallel topstitching.**

## Tech-pack report by style

### VE-FJ-001 - The Firm Jacket

**Role:** Hero product and strongest brand signal.  
**Base sample:** M, black.  
**Silhouette:** Cropped quilted blouson/uniform jacket with structured shoulders, clean body, leather collar, two-way silver metal centre-front zipper, welt-style hand pockets, and straight continuous sleeves.

#### Non-negotiable sleeve-end construction

- Continue the quilted shell and lining straight to the wrist at the sleeve's full finished opening.
- Finish with an approximately 3 cm self-fabric internal turnback.
- Use precise, parallel topstitching; no visible applied cuff piece.
- The sleeve must read as one continuous cylinder from arm to hem, with no inward curve or tightening at the wrist.
- Factory must provide a laid-flat sleeve-opening measurement, front/back fit images, and a close macro photograph of the turnback and stitching.
- Any rib, leather cuff, elastic, gusset, zipper, snap, gather, taper, narrowing, or cinch is a sample failure.

## Shared manufacturing controls

### Labels and brand assets

- Use only the approved VORG-EAVY logo file; never redraw or approximate it.
- Canadian textile labels require accurate fibre content by mass and dealer identity; bilingual presentation is the safest working path pending final compliance review.
- Country of origin, care instructions, size, and dealer information must match the final goods and import records.
- Do not print bulk labels until final composition, testing, dealer identity, and legal copy are approved.

### Sample ladder

1. Fabric/trim swatches, colour lab dips, wash strike-offs, and certificates tied to the exact material.
2. First prototype with factory measurement report and dated construction photographs.
3. Founder/technical review with numbered comments and updated deviation report.
4. Revised fit sample.
5. Size set on the correct women's and men's blocks.
6. Pre-production sample made from bulk-intended fabric, trims, wash, labels, and factory site.
7. Signed sealed sample, approved colour/wash standards, and controlled purchase order.
8. Production evidence and independent pre-shipment inspection before balance release.

No factory should be allowed to skip directly from a marketplace listing or attractive first photo to a bulk order.

## C$6,000 landed cash architecture

The model below is a planning guardrail, not a vendor quote. It treats C$6,000 as the cash needed to get compliant commercial goods to Ottawa, including import GST cash, rather than merely the ex-factory price.

| Cash use | Working allowance |
|---|---:|
| Factory merchandise | C$3,900 |
| Consolidated international freight | C$650 |
| Duty allowance at approximately 17.5% of merchandise | C$683 |
| Import GST cash | C$229 |
| Brokerage | C$120 |
| Independent pre-shipment inspection | C$250 |
| Contingency | C$168 |
| **Total** | **C$6,000** |

Planning used the Bank of Canada 2026-07-31 USD/CAD observation of 1.4029. The reviewed tariff schedules indicate many relevant denim/cotton outerwear classifications near 17% and knit tops/many scarves near 18%, but a licensed customs broker must classify the final materials and constructions. Import GST is generally 5%. These are not permissions to self-classify.

Domestic customer fulfilment, campaign, website, pop-up, returns, and outbound customer shipping are outside this clothing-only landed model. If the actual compliant freight or duty route is more expensive, reduce units or styles. Do not remove inspection, proper declaration, duty, or contingency to make the spreadsheet appear to fit.

## Inventory plan

### Assortment control

- The Firm Jacket: one production colour only.
- Women's denim: one wash only.
- Men's denim: one wash only.
- Women's top: black may advance to bulk; white remains sample-only until opacity and demand evidence clear.
- Scarf: two colours are acceptable only because the style has no size curve and a ready-stock body can pass the quality gate.

### Proof before depth

Before allocating bulk cash, seek at least **40 price-revealed demand actions across the range** and at least **8 product-specific actions for any product being considered at its actual planned price**. A price-revealed action means a deposit, refundable reservation, waitlist choice after seeing price, or equivalent behaviour stronger than a social-media like.

Run 6-10 representative wear testers for each fitted block. Women's denim and men's denim require separate panels. Measure conversion and fit by product and size; do not treat broad “interest” as evidence for a size curve.

### Purchase-order rule

1. Reserve freight, duty, tax cash, brokerage, inspection, and contingency before assigning merchandise units.
2. Fund the two strongest products to useful size depth.
3. Permit a third product only if its demand, MOQ, margin, fit, and timing all fit without weakening the first two.
4. Keep the remaining products sample-only or move them to a later drop.
5. Hold a small defect/exchange reserve in the units that do go to bulk.

Five styles can be sampled. Five fully graded, custom, production-depth styles do not fit this budget cleanly.

## Supplier-vetting protocol

### 1. Identity before product discussion

Obtain the business licence, Chinese unified social credit code or local equivalent, legal Chinese and English names, registered and operating addresses, platform entity, beneficiary bank account, tax/export entity, and named contract signatory. All must reconcile. Request a dated live video walk-through from exterior signage through cutting, sewing, finishing, QC, packing, and warehouse areas.

### 2. Capability and subcontractor map

For each style, require a process-by-process answer showing what is performed in-house and what is subcontracted. For denim, identify pattern, cutting, sewing, washing, drying, finishing, and testing sites. For the jacket, identify quilting, leather cutting/skiving, sewing, finishing, and zipper installation. Refusal to name the wash house or leather subcontractor is disqualifying.

### 3. Evidence and references

Request recent examples similar in material and construction, machine list, monthly capacity by line, two reference customers that can be contacted, third-party assessment reports, and certificates tied to the exact legal entity, address, scope, and valid dates. Verify OEKO-TEX in the label-check database and WRAP on its facility map. Sedex membership is not a certification or a pass/fail quality result.

### 4. Sample discipline

Issue identical RFQs to comparable factories. Record every deviation, date, measurement, construction comment, material substitution, and promised correction. Require actual sample evidence rather than edited catalogue images. A weak prototype is evidence of the supplier's current capability; do not assume bulk will become better automatically.

### 5. Commercial controls

The purchase order must name the exact legal seller and approved production site; attach the controlled tech pack, BOM, POM/tolerances, sealed samples, approved colour/wash standards, inspection standard, packaging, labelling, testing, Incoterm, delivery window, remedies, and a ban on unapproved substitutions or subcontracting. A working payment position is 30% deposit / 70% after passed inspection and shipping-document review, subject to negotiation and enforceability. Avoid personal accounts and off-platform pressure.

### 6. Production inspection

Require production progress photos with batch/lot identification and an independent pre-shipment inspection when at least 80% is packed. Working inspection limits: critical 0, major 2.5, minor 4.0, supplemented by all POMs, garment functions, wash/colour comparison, label, packing, metal detection where appropriate, and jacket sleeve-end verification. The sampling plan and remedy language must be stated in the PO.

### 7. Import and arrival control

Register and configure CARM as required, appoint a Canadian customs broker, confirm tariff classification and importer of record, review commercial invoice/packing list/origin information, and reconcile duty/tax. On arrival, count, quarantine, inspect, and measure before releasing stock for sale.

### Automatic disqualifiers

- Legal name, platform entity, beneficiary account, and licence do not match.
- Refusal of a live dated facility walk-through or independent inspection.
- Hidden wash, leather, print, embroidery, or production subcontractors.
- Pressure to pay for bulk before completing a sample ladder.
- Certificates that cannot be verified against the exact facility and scope.
- Requests for personal, cash, crypto, or undocumented/off-record payments.
- Material or trim substitution without written approval.
- Refusal to distinguish custom MOQ from ready-stock or blank MOQ.

## Shipping and import plan

### Samples

Use a tracked courier on DAP or an equivalent clearly documented term, with true declared commercial value and a proper sample invoice. The objective is speed, traceability, and learning the import process before bulk.

### Bulk quotation method

Request both EXW and FOB prices. Prefer **FOB at a named South China port**, using a buyer-appointed freight forwarder and Canadian customs broker. If two China factories advance, consolidate their inspected cartons at one controlled warehouse.

Get three door-costed routes using exact carton count, gross/net weight, dimensions, and cubic metres:

- courier/express;
- air cargo;
- ocean LCL.

Choose the method backwards from the inspection-ready date and launch buffer, not from the lowest headline freight rate. Treat the current C$650 as a placeholder until exact cargo data is available.

Avoid opaque “tax included” DDP offers when the supplier will not identify the importer of record, customs value, tariff classification, broker, and documents. A cheap route that produces false declarations or no import trail is not acceptable savings.

## What other clothing founders warn against

The external founder reports are anecdotal, not a representative dataset, but the failure modes are repeated and align with standard quality controls:

- approved samples followed by bulk with different length, fit, fabric, or handfeel;
- unapproved fibre blend or gsm changes after deposit;
- over-ordering to chase a lower unit price;
- paying for bulk to “unlock” a sample;
- trusting generic top-factory lists instead of matching a factory to the exact product and process;
- expecting a poor sample to improve automatically in production.

The operating answer is a sealed sample, exact BOM, measurement report, lab evidence, controlled wash/colour standards, written substitution ban, balanced payment terms, and independent inspection. Unit-price discounts do not compensate for unsellable inventory.

## RFQ comparison rule

Every invited supplier must quote the same version and return:

- legal entity, exact production site, and subcontractor map;
- custom MOQ per style, colour, and size, clearly separated from stock MOQ;
- sample cost, sample lead time, revision cost, and refundable/non-refundable terms;
- EXW and FOB price at several quantity breaks;
- fabric/trim specification and supplier/mill availability;
- pattern, grading, wash, label, testing, packaging, and inspection costs;
- production lead time from approved pre-production sample and deposit;
- carton dimensions/weights and named port;
- payment terms, defect remedies, and willingness to accept independent inspection;
- evidence pack answering the vetting stages above.

Compare **landed compliant cost**, evidence quality, sample accuracy, communication discipline, and risk—not the lowest ex-factory quote.

## Approval gates and next move

### Gate A - Founder approval to issue RFQs

Approve only the design/specification directions, country strategy, C$6,000 landed ceiling, and sample budget. This is not bulk authorization.

### Gate B - Desktop supplier verification

At least two comparable candidates per critical product process should answer the same RFQ. Identity, facility, bank, subcontractor, certificate, MOQ, timing, and quotation evidence must be recorded. Any automatic disqualifier removes the supplier.

### Gate C - Sample approval

Pass material, fit, workmanship, measurement, wash/colour, wear-test, and test requirements. Seal the exact approved pre-production sample. A founder's visual approval alone is insufficient if measurements or material evidence fail.

### Gate D - Demand and unit-economics proof

Meet the price-revealed demand thresholds, rebuild the buy from actual MOQs and landed quotes, preserve non-merchandise cash, and rank products. Fund the top two; add a third only if the whole cash and risk model remains sound.

### Gate E - Bulk authorization

Issue a controlled PO only after Gates B-D pass. No supplier currently has this authorization.

### Gate F - Shipment release

Release balance and shipment only after passed independent inspection, document review, broker classification, and freight booking. On arrival, quarantine and inspect before sale.
