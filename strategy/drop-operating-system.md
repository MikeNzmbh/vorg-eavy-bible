# VORG-EAVY Drop Operating System

Checked: 2026-06-25

## Decision Supported

Define a repeatable operating flow that moves VORG-EAVY from item signals to tested product, launch campaign, online sales, pop-up proof, VORG After, and next-city expansion.

This is the investor/team explanation of how the brand compounds: not one lucky drop, but a staged system with gates, owners, proof artifacts, and kill rules.

## Current Decision

The online launch is not password-gated.

Use scarcity through:

- controlled units
- clear release timing
- waitlist and SMS preheat
- local seeding
- pop-up RSVP control
- sold-out archive
- next-city signal capture

Do not block the public online checkout behind a password at launch.

## Operating Thesis

VORG-EAVY should operate as a local-to-national drop machine:

```text
Signal -> Concept -> Sample -> Campaign Proof -> Production -> Campaign Build -> Open Online Drop -> Pop-Up -> VORG After -> City Expansion
```

The loop is designed to answer five questions every cycle:

1. What item deserves attention?
2. What proof says the product should exist?
3. What proof says the campaign can create demand?
4. What proof says the launch made money, culture, or both?
5. Which city or product should receive the next reinvestment?

## Stage Flow

| Stage | Decision | Required Artifacts | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Signal | Is this item worth concepting? | Trend scorecard, source log, audience read, city relevance | Signal / hold / kill |
| 1. Concept | Why this item, why now, why VORG-EAVY? | Product thesis, mood references, role in drop, rough COGS target | Concept approved |
| 2. Sample | Can the idea become a real garment? | Tech pack draft, supplier list, sample order, fit test plan | Sample approved or killed |
| 3. Campaign Proof | Can the story create demand before bulk spend? | Founder table clips, fit clips, styling tests, hook tests, waitlist/SMS signals | Campaign proof score above threshold |
| 4. Production | Can VORG-EAVY order units without fake certainty? | Final quote, MOQ, PP sample or equivalent, QC checklist, label/compliance review | Production approved |
| 5. Campaign Build | Can the drop be made culturally unavoidable? | Trailer, Reels/TikTok calendar, creator briefs, model shoot, product education | Launch campaign locked |
| 6. Open Online Drop | Can the site convert public demand? | Shopify pages, checkout QA, size guide, policies, analytics, email/SMS | Open public launch |
| 7. Pop-Up | Can the brand prove itself physically? | Venue, RSVP, stock plan, staffing, POS, content capture, consent | Pop-up complete |
| 8. VORG After | Can the world extend beyond the transaction? | Themed recap event, night content, next-city tease, city waitlist capture | Next city signal captured |
| 9. Postmortem | What should be reinvested? | Sales, margin, content, return, city, and customer insight report | Next cycle decision |

## Stage Improvements

### 0. Signal

Do not score only "what is trending." Score what has cross-channel evidence and can be made VORG-EAVY.

Improvement:

- Use `research/trend-radar-industry-benchmark.md` as the front-door scoring standard.
- Add a city column: Ottawa/Gatineau now, Montreal/Toronto/Vancouver/Halifax later.
- Separate "heat" from "buy intent."

Kill if:

- The item only has one-source virality.
- The item requires brand noise to feel interesting.
- The item cannot be made distinct without copying a recognizable design.

### 1. Concept

Every item needs a thesis before sample money.

Required answer:

```text
This item belongs in VORG-EAVY because ______.
It serves the drop as ______.
The customer wears it when ______.
It can be shown in content by ______.
The biggest reason to kill it is ______.
```

Improvement:

- Use a one-page concept memo, not a moodboard dump.
- Include the product's campaign angle from day one.

### 2. Sample

Sampling is not just "do we like it?" It is fit, fabric, construction, price, and content proof.

Required sample tests:

- fit on target bodies
- measurement against spec
- movement and comfort
- fabric hand-feel and recovery
- wash or wear-risk notes
- styling with other drop pieces
- phone-camera test and flash-photo test
- table explanation test by founder

Kill or revise if:

- fit cannot be explained clearly
- sample needs too many unpriced corrections
- fabric feels cheaper than the price
- garment does not photograph with authority
- supplier cannot explain construction and correction path

### 3. Campaign Proof

This is the do-or-die stage.

The mistake would be waiting until all inventory is ordered before learning whether the story works. Campaign proof starts with sample content before bulk production.

Required pre-production campaign tests:

- 3 founder table breakdowns: fit, fabric, construction/care.
- 3 model or body-fit clips: front, side, movement, styling.
- 3 styling POV clips: day, night, city.
- 3 social proof tests: poll, waitlist click, DM prompt, or comment prompt.
- 3 hook variants: product-first, founder-first, city-first.

Primary question:

```text
Can the product earn attention, trust, and purchase intent before the expensive campaign is shot?
```

## Campaign Success Probability

This is a VORG-EAVY working model, not an industry-guaranteed success rate.

Use it to estimate whether a campaign is launch-ready:

| Input | Weight | What Counts |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Product clarity | 15 | Can a viewer understand fit, material, price logic, and role in 10 seconds? |
| Founder trust | 15 | Do founder-led explanations make the item feel cared for, not overexplained? |
| Content traction | 20 | Do test posts beat account median view, save, share, comment, or click rates? |
| Local distribution | 15 | Are seeded connectors, creators, campus circles, and event hosts ready? |
| Conversion readiness | 15 | Are product pages, size guide, checkout, email/SMS, and analytics ready? |
| Creative distinctiveness | 10 | Does the campaign have a memorable world, not just clean visuals? |
| Risk control | 10 | Are claims, music, consent, inventory, returns, and event risks controlled? |

Score out of 100:

| Score | Forecast Band | Decision |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 80-100 | 60%-75% chance of hitting internal launch KPIs | Launch with confidence |
| 65-79 | 40%-60% chance | Launch only after fixes or with tighter inventory exposure |
| 50-64 | 25%-40% chance | Delay for a campaign sprint |
| Below 50 | Below 25% chance | Do not launch; rebuild campaign or kill item |

These forecast bands are working assumptions until VORG-EAVY has its own launch history. They should be recalibrated after every drop.

## Campaign Metrics

Use external benchmarks as context, but judge VORG-EAVY mainly against its own account median.

Pre-launch content health:

| Metric | Weak | Healthy | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook retention | Viewers leave immediately | Clear hold in first 3 seconds | Multiple hooks outperform account median |
| View outliers | No post beats median | 10%-20% of posts beat 2x median views | Multiple posts beat 3x median views |
| Saves/shares | Mostly passive likes | Saves or shares appear on product education | Shares and DMs mention buying, fit, city, event |
| Comments | Generic reactions | Specific questions about price, fit, date, sizes | "Where can I buy?" and city/event asks |
| Waitlist/SMS | Low click intent | Clicks rise after product education | Clicks rise after multiple content angles |
| Creator response | One-off reposts | Aligned local creators ask real questions | Creators propose styling, hosting, or event ideas |

Launch funnel health:

| Metric | Reference |
| --- | --- |
| Fashion ecommerce conversion | Littledata reports 1.9% average; 4.3%+ is top 20% in its fashion sample |
| Clothing email click rate | Klaviyo reports 1.83% campaign click rate for clothing and accessories |
| TikTok/Instagram engagement | Socialinsider reports 2026 averages of 3.70% for TikTok and 0.48% for Instagram |
| Posting cadence | Dash Social and Hootsuite both suggest that more posting is not always better; cadence must be tested |

VORG-EAVY should care more about qualified intent than raw views. A post with fewer views but more city, fit, price, and purchase comments can be more valuable than a broad viral post.

The campaign should not be one trailer and hope. It should be a content system.

| Asset Lane | Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trailer | Establish world and emotional pressure | 45-60 second hero film |
| Teasers | Build memory before launch | 6-15 second cuts, date reveal, object closeups |
| Founder table | Build trust and product care | fit explained on table, fabric closeup, construction logic |
| Fit proof | Reduce return risk | model try-on, body movement, size comparison |

## Founder-Led Product Education

The founder-led process should feel like a serious object review, closer to a car walkaround than a sales pitch.

Format:

1. Object on table.
2. Founder hands enter frame.
3. Explain silhouette, material, fit, and construction.
4. Show what changed from sample to final.
5. State one honest tradeoff.
6. Show how to wear it.
7. End with launch date or city signal.

Rules:

- No fake craftsmanship claims.
- No "luxury" claims unless material, finish, and price support them.
- Show care, not desperation.
- Explain fit before hype.
- Let the product carry the authority.

## Open Online Drop

The site should open publicly at launch.

Required states:

- pre-launch waitlist and SMS capture
- open launch with live product pages
- low-stock and sold-out states
- post-launch archive and next-city waitlist

Required pages/modules:

- homepage launch state
- product detail pages
- size and fit guide
- shipping, returns, privacy, terms
- campaign/trailer module
- product education clips
- pop-up RSVP module
- next-city waitlist module

No password gate at checkout.

## Pop-Up

The pop-up is proof, not decoration.

Measure:

- RSVPs
- attendance
- walk-ins
- try-ons
- conversion rate
- average order value
- stock by SKU and size
- city signups
- content captured
- customer objections
- fit/return notes

Improvement:

- Treat daytime as fit/sales/trust.
- Treat nighttime as culture/content/community.
- Keep checkout and stock controlled.
- Capture consent for every usable face-forward asset.

## VORG After

VORG After is the post-launch cultural event. It should not be a vague party.

Purpose:

- create recap content
- celebrate the drop
- deepen local community
- give creators a reason to post
- announce or tease the next city
- capture next-city waitlist demand

Required ending:

```text
Ottawa/Gatineau proved ______.
The next signal we are watching is ______.
Montreal / Toronto / Vancouver / Halifax: join the city list.
```

City expansion should be evidence-based. Do not announce a city as locked until there is enough demand, venue logic, local connector map, and operational capacity.

## Postmortem

Every drop closes with a proof report:

| Area | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Product | What sold, returned, got asked about, or failed fit? |
| Campaign | Which content angle produced qualified demand? |
| Channel | Did TikTok, Reels, email, SMS, creators, or pop-up drive the best signal? |
| Finance | Did sell-through and margin justify the next PO? |
| City | Which city showed the strongest next demand? |
| Team | Which handoffs broke or worked? |
| Investor proof | What can be shown without exaggeration? |
