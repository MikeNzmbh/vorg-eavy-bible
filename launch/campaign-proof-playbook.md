# Campaign Proof Playbook

Checked: 2026-06-25

## Decision Supported

Build the campaign stage of the VORG-EAVY Drop Operating System into a repeatable playbook.

The campaign should create visible demand, cultural pressure, and product trust before and during launch without using fake proof, fake scarcity, or unsafe crowd mechanics.

## Core Rule

Borrow the psychology, not the deception.

The Red Bull empty-can story is useful as a lesson in visible social proof, but it is widely repeated as an unconfirmed or urban-myth style story. Do not plant fake consumption evidence, fake lines, fake customer posts, or fake sell-through.

VORG-EAVY should create real proof that looks unavoidable:

- real people wearing the product
- real founder explanation
- real city sightings
- real RSVP pressure
- real low-stock movement
- real pop-up attendance
- real next-city signups

## Reference Patterns

### Red Bull Pattern

Public marketing retellings say Red Bull supposedly placed empty cans in visible nightlife and campus environments to make the drink look already consumed. One checked source explicitly frames the story as unconfirmed while noting the social-proof principle.
Source: https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/red-bulls-empty-can-strategy-urban-myth-or-marketing-genius/

VORG-EAVY interpretation:

- Do not stage fake consumption.
- Create real public traces of adoption.
- Make the audience feel the product is already moving through the city.

### Corteiz Pattern

Corteiz used surprise physical drops, coordinates, exchanges, scarcity, founder/community gravity, and city movement. Vogue Business reported that Da Great Bolo Exchange drew hundreds of people for 50 jackets after coordinates and instructions were shared. GQ reported the Brooklyn Denim Exchange had participants wear selected denim to the event, hand it in, and leave in Corteiz denim, with collected pairs donated to youth charities.
Sources: https://www.vogue.com/article/corteiz-clint-and-the-future-of-streetwear and https://www.gq.com/story/corteiz-brooklyn-denim-exchange-scene-report

VORG-EAVY interpretation:

- Build participation mechanics, not passive ads.
- Make the city part of the campaign.
- Use exchange, RSVP, proof-of-presence, and founder-led narration.
- Keep safety, venue permission, consent, and crowd control stronger than the reference lane.

## Campaign Objective

Campaign Proof exists to answer:

```text
Can this product earn attention, trust, and purchase intent before VORG-EAVY spends heavily on production, trailer, or event?
```

Campaign Build exists to answer:

```text
Can the approved product be launched with enough story, social proof, and conversion readiness to hit sell-through goals?
```

## Red Lines

Do not build tactics that require:

- fake customers
- fake discarded packaging
- fake lines
- fake sell-outs
- fake founder scarcity claims
- fake testimonials
- undisclosed paid endorsements
- unsafe last-minute crowd surges
- trespassing, unauthorized projection, flyposting, or venue misuse
- littering or staged mess
- use of people's faces without consent

The strongest VORG-EAVY version is aggressive but clean: if explained plainly to a customer, venue, creator, investor, or regulator, the tactic should still sound defensible.

## Tactic Cards

### 1. The Firm Sightings

Objective:

Make the product feel present in the city before the launch page opens.

Mechanic:

- Select 10-20 real local connectors.
- Give each person a sample/final piece or styled prop only if they actually wear it.
- Require no scripted endorsement.
- Capture consented photos or reposts.
- Track location, post URL, DM replies, and waitlist clicks.

Success signal:

- 5+ real sightings.
- 20+ qualified DMs/comments across all sightings.
- At least 2 people ask where/when they can buy.

Kill signal:

- People only react to the person, not the product.
- Product is not visible or understandable.
- Connectors need too much prompting to wear it naturally.

### 2. The Exchange

Objective:

Turn product launch into participation and create material proof of community.

Mechanic:

- RSVP-only, capacity-controlled exchange.
- People bring one clean garment/accessory from a defined category.
- VORG-EAVY offers one of these: limited discount, alteration credit, pop-up priority fitting, or chance to buy a numbered item.
- Collected items are donated, repaired, upcycled, or documented responsibly.

Rules:

- No running.
- No surprise capacity trap.
- Venue approved.
- Security and queue lead present.
- Terms posted before arrival.
- Donation/repair partner confirmed before announcing.

Success signal:

- RSVP fills at least 80% of safe capacity.
- 50%+ attendance from RSVPs.
- Content shows participation without chaos.
- People understand why exchange connects to the product.

Kill signal:

- It becomes a crowd-control risk.
- People misunderstand it as a giveaway.
- No partner exists for collected items.

### 3. The Founder Table

Objective:

Make product care visible and reduce "random clothing brand" skepticism.

Mechanic:

- Object laid flat on table.
- Founder shows fit, material, closures, pockets, labels, seams, and sample changes.
- Each clip answers one buyer anxiety.
- Every claim must be true to sample or final product.

Required clips:

1. Fit: where it sits, who it suits, how to size.
2. Fabric: hand feel, weight, stretch/no stretch, care.
3. Construction: collar, seams, trim, pockets, labels.
4. Corrections: what changed from sample to final.
5. Tradeoff: one honest limitation.

Success signal:

- Saves and shares appear.
- Comments ask size, price, release date, care, or styling.
- Product page click-through rises after education clips.

Kill signal:

- The explanation feels like excuse-making.
- The sample does not support the price.
- Founder cannot explain the garment simply.

### 4. The City Clue Trail

Objective:

Make the city feel activated without causing unsafe rushes.

Mechanic:

- Place approved cards, displays, or QR clues at partner locations.
- Each clue reveals a piece of the drop story, not the full purchase path.
- QR links track city, source, and product interest.

Rules:

- Use permissioned locations only.
- No littering, flyposting, trespass, or unauthorized projection.
- No "first 20 only" messaging unless crowd control is ready.

Success signal:

- QR scans by location.
- Waitlist/SMS conversion by location.
- People repost the clue voluntarily.

Kill signal:

- Location partner is uncomfortable.
- Scans do not convert to qualified intent.
- The clue is confusing without creating product desire.

### 5. The Empty Hanger Wall

Objective:

Show real sell-through during the pop-up.

Mechanic:

- Each hanger or tag represents one real unit.
- As units sell, the hanger/tag is moved to a sold wall.
- The wall is photographed in time-lapse.

Rules:

- Only move hangers for real sales or real holds.
- Mark holds separately from sold.
- Keep SKU/size truth accurate.

Success signal:

- Customers understand scarcity.
- People share the wall.
- It helps explain limited inventory without fake urgency.

Kill signal:

- Staff cannot keep the wall accurate.
- It creates pressure without checkout readiness.

## Campaign Proof Scorecard

Use before production approval and again before launch.

| Category | Weight | Score 0-5 | Evidence Required |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Product clarity | 15 |  | Viewers understand item, fit, material, price logic |
| Founder trust | 15 |  | Table clips produce saves, DMs, or serious questions |
| Real social proof | 15 |  | Sightings, creator posts, RSVPs, organic comments |
| Local activation | 15 |  | Ottawa/Gatineau connectors and locations engaged |
| Content traction | 15 |  | Test posts beat account median or produce qualified intent |
| Conversion path | 10 |  | Waitlist/SMS/product page/checkout tracking ready |
| Risk control | 10 |  | Consent, venue, music, claims, crowd, inventory controlled |
| Next-city signal | 5 |  | City waitlist or poll captures Montreal/Toronto/Vancouver/Halifax |

Formula:

```text
Weighted Score =
  sum((category score / 5) * category weight)
```

Decision:

| Score | Decision |
| ---: | --- |
| 80-100 | Campaign can carry launch |
| 65-79 | Launch after fixing weakest two categories |
| 50-64 | Run a 7-day campaign sprint before launch |
| Below 50 | Do not launch; campaign is not creating enough proof |

## Drop 001 Campaign Sprint

### Sprint 1: Sample Proof

When:

As soon as first credible samples exist.

Output:

- 5 founder table clips.
- 5 fit/movement clips.
- 5 styling clips.
- 3 still-photo sets.
- 1 waitlist/SMS landing page.

Gate:

Do not approve bulk production unless the product can be explained, styled, and photographed with authority.

### Sprint 2: City Proof

When:

After sample corrections are understood.

Output:

- 10 local connector targets.
- 3 permissioned clue locations.
- 1 city waitlist QR.
- 1 pop-up RSVP interest form.
- 1 creator/host shortlist.

Gate:

Do not lock the pop-up concept unless the city response is specific enough to predict attendance.

### Sprint 3: Trailer Proof

When:

After production approval.

Output:

- 45-60 second trailer.
- 6-10 cutdowns.
- 10 product education clips.
- 10 city/social proof clips.
- email/SMS launch sequence.

Gate:

Do not spend heavily on paid distribution until at least three organic or low-budget assets identify a winning angle.

### Sprint 4: Launch And After

When:

Launch week through VORG After.

Output:

- live online drop
- pop-up recap
- empty hanger wall / receipt board proof
- founder thank-you
- sold-out or lessons-learned post
- next-city signal push

Gate:

Postmortem must decide: reorder, evolve, kill, or move city.

## Growth Risk Memo

Objective:

Create visible demand and social proof for VORG-EAVY without relying on fake proof.

Proposed mechanism:

Use real seeding, consented sightings, RSVP exchanges, founder education, controlled pop-up proof, and tracked city waitlists.

Rule surfaces:

- advertising disclosure and creator compensation
- venue permission
- municipal/public-space rules
- privacy and photo consent
- music licensing
- platform policies
- charity/donation representations
- product claims and textile compliance

Opposing argument:

The campaign could look manipulative if it appears to fake demand, create artificial chaos, or imply popularity that does not exist.

Best aggressive path:

Create participatory proof that is real: actual wear, actual RSVPs, actual sell-through, actual donated/exchanged items, actual city signups.

Red lines:

No fake consumption, fake UGC, fake crowds, undisclosed paid endorsements, littering, trespass, or unsafe surprise crowd mechanics.

Supervision model:

Founder approval required before public stunt announcements, exchange mechanics, creator payments, venue posts, donation claims, or city expansion claims.

Evidence required:

- consent records
- creator disclosure notes
- venue approval
- donation/repair partner proof
- QR analytics
- RSVP logs
- sales data
- product/claim approval notes

Risk rating:

Yellow. The upside is high, but execution needs discipline because guerrilla fashion tactics can quickly become brand, safety, or permission risk.

Decision memo:

Approve the real-proof campaign system. Reject fake planted proof. Use Corteiz-style participation and Red Bull-style visibility only when the visible proof is generated by real activity.
